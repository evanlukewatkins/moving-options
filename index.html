🛋️ Option A

Stay put

Do nothing — keep living in Brockley.

By far the cheapest option — £1,345 a month, and a chunk of that is paying down the mortgage rather than disappearing.

No fees, no stamp duty, no upheaval.

The cost is the one that doesn't show up on a bank statement: another few years in a small one-bed with no outdoor space, a long commute, and an area I've outgrown.

🔑 Option B

Keep the flat and rent it out

The obvious idea — let a tenant cover it while I rent somewhere new. Here's why it doesn't work.

Rent coming in£0per month from a tenant
What goes out£0mortgage, agent, insurance, repairs, tax
So I'd lose~£500every month

After 12 months the lender forces me onto a more expensive landlord mortgage — so it gets worse. With my own rent on top: ~£2,750/month all in. Over five years that's roughly £105,000 worse off than staying put.

🔑 Option B · the detail

Where that £2,050 actually goes

A quick breakdown of the monthly outgoings if I let the flat.

Mortgage payment£1,345
Lender's "consent to let" surcharge (they add ~1%)~£200
Letting agent (12% + VAT)~£185
Insurance, safety checks, repairs~£100
Tax on the rent (at my rate)~£220
Going out each month~£2,050
Coming in from tenant£1,550
So the flat costs me~£500 / month

And that's year one. After 12 months I'd have to switch to a proper landlord mortgage at a higher rate — the loss grows.

The lender also won't let me borrow that much against £1,550 of rent, so I might not even qualify without paying down a chunk of the mortgage first.

The mortgage penalty

So I need to sell — but there's a catch

My mortgage deal runs to 2029. Leaving early means a fee.

Which leaves the two real ways forward.

📦 Option C

Sell the flat and rent

Maximum flexibility — fresh start straight away.

Walk away with about £96,000–£104,000 in the bank, depending on whether I go now or next summer.

Rent somewhere modern — outdoor space, gym, parking — for around £2,250 a month.

Easy to move again if life changes. But rent is just gone each month — and if I bought again later I'd pay stamp duty on top of the penalty, so it can end up costing twice.

Monthly£2,250
Penalty fee£8k–£14k
Cash freed up~£100k
5-year cost vs staying~£95k–£125k
🏡 Option D

Sell and buy somewhere bigger

Move my mortgage across to the new place — no penalty fee at all.

Because I'm moving the mortgage rather than ending it, the £14,000 penalty disappears.

Put my Brockley equity down as a deposit and buy at around £550,000 — a proper two-bed with a balcony, parking and a gym, somewhere greener.

Monthly is about £2,200 — almost the same as renting — but it's going into something I own.

Monthly~£2,200
Penalty fee£0
Stamp duty~£17,500
5-year cost vs staying~£47k
